Mathematical Journey

I did well in mathematics competitions and examinations. Yet, over time, I became increasingly dissatisfied with the way I was learning mathematics.

Much of my training had taught me to recognize familiar patterns, recall the appropriate technique, and reproduce it efficiently. I became good at solving problems that fit within the mathematical machinery I had already learned, but had comparatively little experience with harder questions:

*How does one discover a proof? How does one recognize the underlying structure of an unfamiliar problem? How are mathematical definitions invented? What does one do when there is no known template to apply?*

These questions eventually led to a more fundamental realization: **I wanted to learn not only how to consume mathematics, but how to think mathematically.**

Since then, I have been deliberately rebuilding my mathematical education from a different perspective. I revisit subjects I have studied before, but now with an emphasis on proofs, problem-solving, experimentation, abstraction, counterexamples, generalization, and understanding why mathematical ideas were created in the first place.

I am particularly interested in the boundary between mathematics and computer science: using existing mathematical structures to solve problems, recognizing structure in unfamiliar situations, and eventually learning how new mathematical structures and ideas can be created when existing ones are insufficient.

This is therefore not an attempt to “start mathematics over.” It is an attempt to learn a different way of doing mathematics.
